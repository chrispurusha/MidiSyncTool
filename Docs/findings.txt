2026-09-02  A HOST THAT LIES THE WAY ABLETON LIES
------------------------------------------------------------
tools/mstDriver - a headless VST3 host that hands the plug-in a ProcessContext modelled on the
measured behaviour of Live 12.4.5, so clock work can be developed and regression-tested without a
DAW and without CT in front of one.

DELIBERATELY NOT A CORRECT HOST. An idealised one would be worse than useless: it would let a
scheduler be written against continuous musical time, which is the exact assumption Live breaks. So
it reproduces the faults:
  - systemTime always 0 and kSystemTimeValid never set, whatever the plug-in asks for
  - continousTimeSamples always 0
  - the block is never split at a loop boundary
  - AT A WRAP the report LAGS BY ONE BLOCK: the block after the wrap reads exactly the loop start,
    and the one after that reads the remainder the wrap actually left - not remainder-plus-a-block

That last point took two attempts. The first version advanced the position through the snap block,
which put the harness a whole block ahead of Live - and a scheduler developed against it would have
come out systematically early by up to 5.3 ms at 130 BPM, which is precisely the class of defect
this tool is meant to detect in other people's equipment.

VERIFIED AGAINST THE CAPTURE, 130 BPM, 1-bar loop, 256-frame blocks at 48 kHz:

                        Live (measured)          mstDriver
  block before wrap     ppq 3.998222             ppq 3.998222
  snap block            0.000000, smp 0          0.000000, smp 0
  block after           0.009750, smp 216        0.009778, smp 217
  second wrap           3.996417 -> 0 ->         3.996444 -> 0 ->
                        0.007944, smp 176        0.008000, smp 177

Within one sample throughout, and it reproduces the drift between successive wraps (3.998, 3.996,
3.994) that comes of a loop length which is not a whole number of blocks.

WHAT IT DOES NOT MODEL YET: tempo ramps (Live reports tempo per block and ppq stays continuous
across a change - worth adding when the estimator is written), transport start/stop, playhead jumps
while stopped, and buffer size changes.


2026-09-02  WHAT LIVE DOES AT A LOOP WRAP - AND WHY IT MATTERS
------------------------------------------------------------
Second capture, with a 1-bar loop at 130 BPM and tempo changes, after a reload. 35 discontinuities.

THE BLOCK IS NEVER SPLIT. n=256 on every single line, wrap or no wrap. Live does not hand a short
block at the loop boundary, so a wrap always falls INSIDE a block. That was worth checking, because
if it had split, a tick scheduler could have treated every block as musically contiguous.

WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENS, and it is not continuous:

    JUMP  ppq 3.998222 -> 0.000000  (expected 4.007972)  smp 0
    JUMP  ppq 0.000000 -> 0.009750  (expected 0.011556)  smp 216

  At 130 BPM a quarter note is 22153.8 samples and a 256-frame block is 0.011556 QN - which is
  exactly what the prediction says, so the arithmetic is sound. Then:
    - the block starting at 3.998222 would have ended at 4.009778, i.e. 39.4 samples PAST the loop
      end at 4.0
    - the next block reports ppq EXACTLY 0.000000 and projectTimeSamples EXACTLY 0
    - the block after that reports 0.009750 QN / 216 samples - and 256 - 39.4 = 216.6

  So Live reports one block AT the loop start regardless of where inside the block the wrap actually
  fell, and the true sub-block phase only shows up in the FOLLOWING block. projectTimeSamples snaps
  the same way, so the two agree with each other and both lie in the same way.

  THE CONSEQUENCE FOR THE SCHEDULER: the exact sample at which the loop wrapped CANNOT be read from
  projectTimeMusic. It has to be inferred - (previous ppq + one block) minus the loop end, converted
  to samples. Assuming continuity across a wrap would put every tick after it out by up to a block,
  which at 130 BPM is 5.3 ms, and it would be a systematic error rather than jitter.

OUR OWN CLOCK WORKS. With systemTime unavailable (see the previous entry), AudioGetCurrentHostTime()
read at the top of process() advances by "wall +5.33 ms" line after line - 256/48000 is 5.333 ms
exactly. Steady enough to schedule against, which is the thing that had to be true for the
replacement plan to stand up.

TEMPO CHANGES are reported per block and ppq stays continuous across them (105 -> 114 -> 117 -> 118
in one capture). The small JUMP lines with deltas around 1e-4 are tempo ramps, not discontinuities:
the prediction uses the previous block's tempo and the tempo moved. The 1e-6 tolerance is too tight
and turns a ramp into twenty log lines - it should scale with a fraction of a block.

kCycleActive appears as CYCLE in the flags whenever loop is enabled, so the plug-in can tell that a
wrap is even possible before one happens.


2026-09-02  WHAT ABLETON ACTUALLY GIVES US - AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
------------------------------------------------------------
First plug-in, loaded on an audio track in Live 12.4.5 at 105 BPM, 4/4, 48 kHz, 256-frame blocks.
49 logged lines covering stopped, playing, a playhead jump and stopped again.

WORKS, and the design depends on all of it:
  - processContext is NOT null. IProcessContextRequirements did its job; asking for it on the first
    commit was the right call and a plug-in that skipped it would have got nothing.
  - kTempoValid, kProjectTimeMusicValid, kBarPositionValid, kTimeSigValid and the transport state
    are all set, every block.
  - tempo and ppq are EXACTLY self-consistent. At 105 BPM the heartbeat two seconds apart advances
    ppq by 3.5000 every time - 105/60 * 2 = 3.5 quarter notes. No rounding drift over the capture.
  - projectTimeSamples is exact and matches: ppq 3.5 at sample 96000, which is 2.0 s at 48 kHz.
  - barPositionMusic is the ppq of the current bar's start (144.0 when ppq is 146.0, i.e. bar 37 of
    4/4). Useful directly for Song Position Pointer.

DOES NOT WORK, AND IT IS THE ONE THE WHOLE SCHEDULING PLAN RESTED ON:

  *** ProcessContext.systemTime IS ALWAYS ZERO, AND kSystemTimeValid IS NEVER SET. ***

  All 49 lines: "sysTime 0", and the flag list reads "tempo ptMusic bar timeSig" with no sysTime -
  despite kNeedSystemTime being requested. continousTimeSamples is likewise always 0.

  design.md said: read systemTime, convert with AudioConvertNanosToHostTime(), hand CoreMIDI packets
  stamped in the future. That is not available. The replacement is to call AudioGetCurrentHostTime()
  OURSELVES at the top of process() - which gives the wall clock at the moment the block is being
  computed, NOT the moment its audio will be heard. The difference is the output latency: unknown,
  but CONSTANT for a given device and buffer size.

  So the architecture survives and one thing changes character: the round-trip measurement stops
  being a nicety for section 4 and becomes LOAD-BEARING, because it is what turns "now, as the CPU
  sees it" into "now, as the listener hears it". Everything else - ppq, tempo, sample position - is
  solid enough to schedule against once that constant is known.

STILL UNKNOWN: what a LOOP WRAP looks like. The capture contains one big discontinuity (ppq 10.5 ->
146.0 between two heartbeats) from a playhead move, but the 2-second heartbeat is far too coarse to
show what Live does at a loop boundary - which CT flags as the tricky part. The logging needs to
fire ON DISCONTINUITY, not on a timer, before that question can be answered.
