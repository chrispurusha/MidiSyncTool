2026-09-02  CLOCK GENERATES CORRECTLY - AND NEEDS A LOOKAHEAD
------------------------------------------------------------
src/msClock.c generating 24 PPQN from the host's ppq, sent through src/msMidi.c with a scheduled
timestamp, driven offline by tools/mstDriver at 130 BPM with a 1-bar loop.

CORRECT, on the musical side:
  - 419 ticks in 8 s = 52.4/s, and 130 BPM x 24 PPQN is 52.0 - right rate, right tempo.
  - The wrap counter increments exactly once per loop, so the correction in ms_clock_process() is
    firing on Live's snap-to-loop-start pattern rather than being fooled by it.
  - Tempest appears in the destination list (index 7 here), so the intended first target is
    reachable without any further work.

NOT YET GOOD ENOUGH, on delivery. Measured through IAC, target 19.2308 ms between ticks:
                                     mean         RMS        worst
  raw timestamp test (no plug-in)  -0.0003 ms   0.019 ms   +0.054 ms
  clock through the plug-in        -0.1307 ms   1.667 ms  -18.050 ms

  The mean is fine, so nothing is drifting. The RMS and that -18 ms worst case are BUNCHING: a tick
  is currently scheduled for "this block's wall time plus its offset within the block", so if a
  block is picked up late, some of its ticks are already in the past when they are submitted and
  CoreMIDI delivers them at once.

  THE FIX WAS NOT A LOOKAHEAD, though that was the first guess and it was wrong. Adding one changed
  nothing (1.667 -> 1.711 ms RMS), which is what sent the investigation somewhere better: printing
  WHERE the outliers fell rather than how big they were.

  They landed 96, 96, 96 ticks apart. 96 ticks is exactly one bar of 4/4 at 24 PPQN - so it was the
  LOOP WRAP every time, and structural rather than noise. The signature was a ~2 ms interval
  followed by a long one: a DOUBLED TICK. The tick at the loop END and the tick at the loop START
  are the same musical moment, and a generator that searches for every 1/24 boundary inside each
  block's musical span emits both.

  Replacing that search with a PHASE ACCUMULATOR fixed it. Carrying the leftover fraction of a tick
  from block to block cannot revisit a boundary, so it cannot duplicate one, wrap or no wrap:

                              mean         RMS        worst      outlier spacing
    range search           -0.131 ms   1.711 ms  -18.019 ms      96, 96, 96 (structural)
    phase accumulator      +0.002 ms   0.922 ms   -2.503 ms      54, 45 (scattered)

  Tick count came out at 519 against the 520 the tempo predicts, so nothing is being dropped either.

  THE LOOKAHEAD IS STILL THERE AND IS STILL UNJUSTIFIED. Swept at 5 / 10 / 30 ms it gave 0.936 /
  0.891 / 0.901 ms RMS - no effect at all. It is kept at 10 ms because the failure it guards against
  is real in principle and costs only a known constant, but nothing here has demonstrated it buys
  anything, and this harness may be incapable of showing it. Revisit against a real host under load.

  THE REMAINING 0.9 ms IS PROBABLY THE HARNESS, NOT THE PLUG-IN. The raw timestamp test, with no
  plug-in and no block pacing, reached 0.019 ms through the same port. The difference is that
  mstDriver paces blocks with usleep, so the blockHostTime every tick is placed against is itself
  jittering. That claim is untested; Live is the place to settle it.

TWO HARNESS BUGS FOUND BY THIS, BOTH MINE, both of which produced numbers that looked like plug-in
defects:
  1. --realtime slept one block's length per block. usleep always overshoots, so the harness ran 20%
     slow (wall +6.4 ms against 5.33), dragging every scheduled tick with it: a +3.27 ms MEAN error
     that was entirely the harness. Now sleeps until block N is DUE, against an absolute start.
  2. The clock listener matched source names one way round only. IAC's destination is "IAC Driver
     Bus 1" and its source is just "Bus 1", so it connected to nothing and reported no measurement -
     which reads as "the clock never arrived".

Worth keeping in mind for everything that follows: this harness is a sleeping loop pretending to be
an audio callback, and its own timing is nowhere near a real host's. Delivery figures from it are
indicative. The number that counts comes from Live.


2026-09-02  CALIBRATION RIG - HOW THE TEMPEST IS WIRED
------------------------------------------------------------
CT's setup for the first real calibration, recorded before it is needed:
  - Dave Smith TEMPEST, connected by USB MIDI, appears directly in the CoreMIDI destination list
    (index 7 in the enumeration above) - no interface in the way.
  - Its audio returns on QU-24 inputs 15 and 16 (14 and 15 zero-based).

"no audio on those inputs", "no transients detected", "the transients are not on a grid" - rather

measurement code, because it changes what the measurement has to report.


2026-09-02  SCHEDULED MIDI IS 220x TIGHTER THAN IMMEDIATE - MEASURED
------------------------------------------------------------
tools/midiTimestampTest.c. 200 clock bytes at 20.8333 ms apart (24 PPQN at 120 BPM) down IAC Driver
Bus 1 and received on its own source, timed on arrival.

                          mean error      RMS        worst
  SCHEDULED ahead         -0.0003 ms   0.019 ms   +0.054 ms
  IMMEDIATE (ts 0)        +3.8703 ms   4.157 ms   +6.871 ms

CoreMIDI honours a future timestamp, and honours it well: 19 microseconds RMS with no systematic
drift. Sending with timestamp 0 from a sleeping loop - the shape of a generator that emits at block
boundaries instead of scheduling - is 220 times worse on RMS AND carries a 3.9 ms MEAN error,
because a sleep always overshoots and the error accumulates rather than averaging out.

So the design decision is settled with a number rather than an argument: hand CoreMIDI the future
time and let the driver place it.

A TRAP ON THE WAY, worth recording because the first result was a lie. The receive callback
originally recorded the PACKET'S OWN timestamp, which IAC passes through untouched - so it measured
whether CoreMIDI can copy a number back, and duly reported RMS 0.0000 ms. A perfect result that
flattered the design and meant nothing. The arrival time has to be read from the wall clock in the
callback. Any future measurement through a virtual port needs the same care.

CAVEAT: IAC is an in-memory virtual driver. A real DIN interface adds its own jitter, and the wire
itself is about 320 microseconds per byte at 31250 baud - so the interface will dominate in
practice. What this establishes is that OUR contribution sits far below anything we would be trying
to measure in someone else's equipment.


2026-09-02  A HOST THAT LIES THE WAY ABLETON LIES
------------------------------------------------------------
tools/mstDriver - a headless VST3 host that hands the plug-in a ProcessContext modelled on the
measured behaviour of Live 12.4.5, so clock work can be developed and regression-tested without a
DAW and without CT in front of one.

DELIBERATELY NOT A CORRECT HOST. An idealised one would be worse than useless: it would let a
scheduler be written against continuous musical time, which is the exact assumption Live breaks. So
it reproduces the faults:
  - systemTime always 0 and kSystemTimeValid never set, whatever the plug-in asks for
  - continousTimeSamples always 0
  - the block is never split at a loop boundary
  - AT A WRAP the report LAGS BY ONE BLOCK: the block after the wrap reads exactly the loop start,
    and the one after that reads the remainder the wrap actually left - not remainder-plus-a-block

That last point took two attempts. The first version advanced the position through the snap block,
which put the harness a whole block ahead of Live - and a scheduler developed against it would have
come out systematically early by up to 5.3 ms at 130 BPM, which is precisely the class of defect
this tool is meant to detect in other people's equipment.

VERIFIED AGAINST THE CAPTURE, 130 BPM, 1-bar loop, 256-frame blocks at 48 kHz:

                        Live (measured)          mstDriver
  block before wrap     ppq 3.998222             ppq 3.998222
  snap block            0.000000, smp 0          0.000000, smp 0
  block after           0.009750, smp 216        0.009778, smp 217
  second wrap           3.996417 -> 0 ->         3.996444 -> 0 ->
                        0.007944, smp 176        0.008000, smp 177

Within one sample throughout, and it reproduces the drift between successive wraps (3.998, 3.996,
3.994) that comes of a loop length which is not a whole number of blocks.

WHAT IT DOES NOT MODEL YET: tempo ramps (Live reports tempo per block and ppq stays continuous
across a change - worth adding when the estimator is written), transport start/stop, playhead jumps
while stopped, and buffer size changes.


2026-09-02  WHAT LIVE DOES AT A LOOP WRAP - AND WHY IT MATTERS
------------------------------------------------------------
Second capture, with a 1-bar loop at 130 BPM and tempo changes, after a reload. 35 discontinuities.

THE BLOCK IS NEVER SPLIT. n=256 on every single line, wrap or no wrap. Live does not hand a short
block at the loop boundary, so a wrap always falls INSIDE a block. That was worth checking, because
if it had split, a tick scheduler could have treated every block as musically contiguous.

WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENS, and it is not continuous:

    JUMP  ppq 3.998222 -> 0.000000  (expected 4.007972)  smp 0
    JUMP  ppq 0.000000 -> 0.009750  (expected 0.011556)  smp 216

  At 130 BPM a quarter note is 22153.8 samples and a 256-frame block is 0.011556 QN - which is
  exactly what the prediction says, so the arithmetic is sound. Then:
    - the block starting at 3.998222 would have ended at 4.009778, i.e. 39.4 samples PAST the loop
      end at 4.0
    - the next block reports ppq EXACTLY 0.000000 and projectTimeSamples EXACTLY 0
    - the block after that reports 0.009750 QN / 216 samples - and 256 - 39.4 = 216.6

  So Live reports one block AT the loop start regardless of where inside the block the wrap actually
  fell, and the true sub-block phase only shows up in the FOLLOWING block. projectTimeSamples snaps
  the same way, so the two agree with each other and both lie in the same way.

  THE CONSEQUENCE FOR THE SCHEDULER: the exact sample at which the loop wrapped CANNOT be read from
  projectTimeMusic. It has to be inferred - (previous ppq + one block) minus the loop end, converted
  to samples. Assuming continuity across a wrap would put every tick after it out by up to a block,
  which at 130 BPM is 5.3 ms, and it would be a systematic error rather than jitter.

OUR OWN CLOCK WORKS. With systemTime unavailable (see the previous entry), AudioGetCurrentHostTime()
read at the top of process() advances by "wall +5.33 ms" line after line - 256/48000 is 5.333 ms
exactly. Steady enough to schedule against, which is the thing that had to be true for the
replacement plan to stand up.

TEMPO CHANGES are reported per block and ppq stays continuous across them (105 -> 114 -> 117 -> 118
in one capture). The small JUMP lines with deltas around 1e-4 are tempo ramps, not discontinuities:
the prediction uses the previous block's tempo and the tempo moved. The 1e-6 tolerance is too tight
and turns a ramp into twenty log lines - it should scale with a fraction of a block.

kCycleActive appears as CYCLE in the flags whenever loop is enabled, so the plug-in can tell that a
wrap is even possible before one happens.


2026-09-02  WHAT ABLETON ACTUALLY GIVES US - AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
------------------------------------------------------------
First plug-in, loaded on an audio track in Live 12.4.5 at 105 BPM, 4/4, 48 kHz, 256-frame blocks.
49 logged lines covering stopped, playing, a playhead jump and stopped again.

WORKS, and the design depends on all of it:
  - processContext is NOT null. IProcessContextRequirements did its job; asking for it on the first
    commit was the right call and a plug-in that skipped it would have got nothing.
  - kTempoValid, kProjectTimeMusicValid, kBarPositionValid, kTimeSigValid and the transport state
    are all set, every block.
  - tempo and ppq are EXACTLY self-consistent. At 105 BPM the heartbeat two seconds apart advances
    ppq by 3.5000 every time - 105/60 * 2 = 3.5 quarter notes. No rounding drift over the capture.
  - projectTimeSamples is exact and matches: ppq 3.5 at sample 96000, which is 2.0 s at 48 kHz.
  - barPositionMusic is the ppq of the current bar's start (144.0 when ppq is 146.0, i.e. bar 37 of
    4/4). Useful directly for Song Position Pointer.

DOES NOT WORK, AND IT IS THE ONE THE WHOLE SCHEDULING PLAN RESTED ON:

  *** ProcessContext.systemTime IS ALWAYS ZERO, AND kSystemTimeValid IS NEVER SET. ***

  All 49 lines: "sysTime 0", and the flag list reads "tempo ptMusic bar timeSig" with no sysTime -
  despite kNeedSystemTime being requested. continousTimeSamples is likewise always 0.

  design.md said: read systemTime, convert with AudioConvertNanosToHostTime(), hand CoreMIDI packets
  stamped in the future. That is not available. The replacement is to call AudioGetCurrentHostTime()
  OURSELVES at the top of process() - which gives the wall clock at the moment the block is being
  computed, NOT the moment its audio will be heard. The difference is the output latency: unknown,
  but CONSTANT for a given device and buffer size.

  So the architecture survives and one thing changes character: the round-trip measurement stops
  being a nicety for section 4 and becomes LOAD-BEARING, because it is what turns "now, as the CPU
  sees it" into "now, as the listener hears it". Everything else - ppq, tempo, sample position - is
  solid enough to schedule against once that constant is known.

STILL UNKNOWN: what a LOOP WRAP looks like. The capture contains one big discontinuity (ppq 10.5 ->
146.0 between two heartbeats) from a playhead move, but the 2-second heartbeat is far too coarse to
show what Live does at a loop boundary - which CT flags as the tricky part. The logging needs to
fire ON DISCONTINUITY, not on a timer, before that question can be answered.

2026-09-02  THE 0.92 ms JITTER WAS THE HARNESS, ENTIRELY - measured, not argued
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
mstDriver paced its blocks with usleep against an absolute deadline. That fixed the 20% drag but
not the per-block scatter, and the scatter went straight into blockHostTime, from which every tick
is stamped. The suspicion was recorded in to-test.txt as "probably mostly harness". It was all of it.

The same plug-in, same IAC destination, same 130 BPM, same 24 PPQN, differing only in what paced
the blocks:

                              block period RMS      delivered interval RMS      worst
  usleep loop, 256 frames         1.05 ms                0.97 ms             -2.53 ms
  QU-24 callback, 512 frames      0.019 ms               0.033 ms            -0.14 ms

A factor of 30. The raw timestamp test in tools/midiTimestampTest.c had already shown 0.019 ms with
no block pacing at all, so 0.033 ms from a real device callback is essentially that number plus the
device's own callback scatter - the generator is not adding anything measurable.

The lesson generalises past this project: a harness that paces with usleep cannot measure anything
finer than usleep's overshoot, and it will attribute its own overshoot to whatever it is testing.
Twice now that has produced a figure that looked like a defect in the plug-in - first a +3.27 ms
mean, now a 0.92 ms RMS - and both times the plug-in was innocent.

COMMIT MARGIN, the number that replaces the guessing about MS_LOOKAHEAD_MS. The plug-in now measures
how much future was left on each tick at the moment it was handed to CoreMIDI (msStats.c). Against
the QU-24 at a 512-frame buffer: mean +15.3 ms, minimum +10.04 ms, 0 late out of 332. The minimum
sits exactly at the 10 ms lookahead, which says the block was never late enough to eat into it. So
offline, on an idle machine, the lookahead is still unjustified - but the instrument to justify or
retire it now exists and reports from inside a real host under real load, which the offline sweep
never could.

DRIFT MUST BE COUNTED FROM THE TICKS, NOT FROM projectTimeMusic. The first version of the telemetry
divided reported musical position by wall time, and against a one-bar loop it reported 9.3 BPM
against a host tempo of 130 - because ppq goes backwards at every wrap. The tick count is monotonic
by construction and is also the thing actually being put on the wire.

THE HARNESS NOW RUNS REAL AUDIO. mstDriver --audio NAME opens a CoreAudio device (GenBridge's
device.c and ring.c, copied verbatim into src/ as msDevice/msRing), takes the plug-in's input from
--in-ch and writes its output to --out-ch. This is what section 3 of the concept needs: the plug-in
is an Fx precisely so it can hear the hardware answer its own clock, and none of that can be
developed against silence. Verified against the QU-24 on 2026-09-02: input from channel 14 (Tempest,
15/16 on the desk) read -62 dBFS of noise floor with the machine silent, 0 ring overflows and 0
underruns over 750 blocks.

TRANSPORT, verified offline. Start when play begins at zero; SPP followed by Continue when it begins
anywhere else - beat 128 for bar 8, MIDI beats being sixteenths; Stop on the falling edge, once. The
harness's listener prints them in arrival order, because an SPP that lands after its Continue is
useless and only the order shows that.

2026-09-02  TRANSIENT DETECTOR, and what its number does and does not contain
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
msDetect.c: one-pole high pass at 150 Hz, instant-attack envelope with a 15 ms release, a 400 ms
noise-floor follower, and an onset declared when the envelope clears the greater of 4x that floor
and -56 dBFS, with a 40 ms refractory. Each onset is matched to the OLDEST outstanding scheduled
tick within 300 ms - oldest rather than nearest, because a device whose latency approaches the
spacing between hits would otherwise match each transient to the NEXT hit and report a latency near
zero, which is both wrong and flattering.

VALIDATED WITHOUT HARDWARE. mstDriver --inject-ms D writes a click into the input buffer D ms after
every quarter note. The expected reading is D minus MS_LOOKAHEAD_MS, because the clock stamps a tick
10 ms ahead and that is genuinely when CoreMIDI puts it on the wire, so the latency is measured from
the stamp:

  --inject-ms 20, usleep pacing      mean 10.006 ms   jitter RMS 0.87 ms
  --inject-ms 20, QU-24 pacing       mean  9.997 ms   jitter RMS 0.038 ms

Against a prediction of exactly 10.000. The detector's own contribution to the jitter figure is
therefore about 38 microseconds, which is nothing against any drum machine - and once again the
0.87 ms was the sleeping harness rather than the code.

THE FIRST TICK NOW LANDS ON THE DOWNBEAT, at the same instant as the Start. Leaving phase at zero
put it one full tick later - 19.2 ms at 130 BPM. That is defensible by the standard and useless
here: gear differs on whether step 1 fires on the Start byte or on the first clock after it, and the
two readings would have differed by exactly that 19.2 ms with nothing to say which was right.

WHAT THE FIGURE CONTAINS. It is a ROUND TRIP: tick stamp, to transient in a buffer this plug-in was
handed. That includes the drum machine's MIDI-in to audio-out delay, which is the number worth
reporting, plus the interface's A/D, the host's input buffering and the lookahead. The extra terms
are constant for a rig and buffer size, so they are subtractable once measured - msDevice.c already
enumerates them. Until they are, the log says "round trip" and not "device latency". The JITTER
figure needs no such apology: every constant cancels in a deviation.

2026-09-02  FIRST REAL MEASUREMENT - Tempest over USB MIDI, audio via QU-24 15/16
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NOTE PATH, the note probe with the Tempest's own sequencer idle. Forty notes, C1 (note 36 - that is
what Ableton calls C1, confirmed by the hardware answering it), velocity 110, 400 ms apart:

  round trip mean 21.94 ms, min 21.60, max 22.29, jitter RMS 0.162 ms, peak deviation 0.353 ms
  40 hits, 0 missed, 0 spurious - repeatable across runs to about 0.05 ms on the mean

That figure is a ROUND TRIP and contains the QU-24's A/D and a 512-frame input buffer (10.67 ms)
as well as the Tempest. The jitter figure contains none of that, because constants cancel.

CLOCK PATH: nothing. The Tempest receives Start and clock - the destination resolves, the transport
logs, the interval measures 0.033 ms RMS on the wire - and produces silence, 42 missed and an input
peak of -66 dBFS. It is not following external sync. Left with CT to check.

THE DETECTOR NEEDED THREE FIXES, and the first two were wrong repairs that the third replaced:

1. A time-only refractory. The kick was still sounding when the 40 ms gate lifted, giving exactly one
   spurious onset per hit at 40.0 to 46.3 ms - several at exactly the refractory, which is what said
   the envelope had never come down.
2. Hysteresis against the hit's own peak, plus freezing the noise floor during a hit so the drum
   could not raise its own bar. This moved the retrigger from 44 ms to 202 ms but did not remove it:
   captured and plotted, the kick has no second attack at all, just a long ripply tail hovering
   around the absolute floor for 300 ms and re-crossing it.
3. WHAT ACTUALLY WORKED: the detector only looks for a transient while one is DUE. This is a
   calibration instrument and it knows exactly when it expects a hit; outside those windows a rising
   edge is not a measurement of anything. 40 hits, 0 missed, 0 spurious, and no threshold anywhere
   was tuned to this drum, this desk or this gain - which the alternative repair would have been.

LATENCY COMPENSATION now exists: ms_midi_set_offset_ms(), applied inside ms_midi_send_at so it
reaches ticks, transport and probe notes alike and cannot put them out of step with each other.
Negative sends earlier. Proved against the harness's --echo-ms: a synthetic 7.5 ms device measured
7.58 ms and compensating by that figure brought it to 0.6 ms.

AND IT GIVES MS_LOOKAHEAD_MS ITS JUSTIFICATION AT LAST. An event cannot be sent earlier than the
block it was decided in, so the lookahead is the headroom the compensation spends - compensating
12 ms with 10 ms of lookahead cannot work, and the commit-margin telemetry says so by going
negative. The lookahead is not overhead, it is the budget.

TWO TOOLS THAT PAID FOR THEMSELVES IMMEDIATELY. tools/msLevels prints the peak on every input a
device has, which answered "is it arriving where you think" in five seconds - the question that cost
EmuUtility a whole session. tools/msCapture dumps one channel to raw floats, which is what settled
the 202 ms retrigger: the waveform showed there was no second hit, only a tail.

2026-09-02  THE CLOCK PATH, once the Tempest was actually slaved (its sync source was not USB)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  NOTE path  (probe C1, its sequencer idle)   mean 21.94 ms   jitter RMS 0.162 ms   40/40
  CLOCK path (its own sequencer, quarters)    mean 21.94 ms   jitter RMS 0.169 ms   42/42

IDENTICAL, to a thousandth of a millisecond on the mean. The difference between the two paths is
what the drum machine's own sequencer contributes, and on this machine it contributes NOTHING
measurable - its clock handling is as tight as its note handling. That is a real result about the
device, and it is only available because the two paths are measured separately.

Both figures are round trips and both contain the QU-24's A/D and a 512-frame input buffer.

COMPENSATION IS A PHASE ADVANCE, NOT AN EARLIER SEND. The obvious mechanism - subtract the measured
latency from each timestamp - cannot work, and the reason is arithmetic rather than tuning. A tick
for musical moment m is stamped at wall(m) + L and sounds at wall(m) + L + D, so landing it on m
needs the stamp at wall(m) - D: earlier than the block that decided it. Raising L raises the
requirement by exactly as much.

Measured, rather than argued. MST_OFFSET_MS=-21.9 against a 10 ms lead pinned every tick to the
present and took jitter from 0.169 ms RMS to 3.220 - it turned a clean clock into a bunched one,
which is precisely the defect this tool exists to remove.

The mechanism that works costs nothing, because the tick stream is periodic: a grid shifted earlier
by L + D is indistinguishable from one whose PHASE is advanced by that much, and phase is free to
move. Applied on hardware at 21.9 ms, the commit margin was unchanged (min +10.038 ms, 0 late of
832) and the clock stayed at 0.188 ms RMS. The advance is tracked incrementally rather than
reapplied, because it is a fixed number of milliseconds and therefore a changing number of quarter
notes - a tempo change must move the phase by the difference only.

The one casualty is the first L + D of a run: the grid cannot be advanced before the transport has
started, so the opening beat is uncompensated. Every beat after it is exact.

NOT YET VERIFIED END TO END. The measured round trip is unaffected by compensation, correctly - the
detector times from the stamp, so it keeps reporting the device's latency whatever is being done
about it. Proving the sound now lands ON the beat needs an independent reference: the host's own
audio. Patching QU-24 output 29 back to an input would supply it, and would pin the constant part of
the 21.94 ms at the same time.

2026-09-02  THE PANEL
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built on GenBridge's editor, which is now the house pattern for a plug-in panel: SynthLib's renderer
rather than AppKit widgets (so it cannot drift from the applications' look without the change being
visible in all of them), a Metal-backed NSView on a 30 Hz timer, and an IPlugView wrapper that is the
only Objective-C++ in the project.

WHAT IS SHOWN, in four sections: MASTER (host tempo, transport, position), MIDI OUTPUT (port, clocks
sent, commit margin and its worst case, late clocks, host block jitter), MEASURED DEVICE (round trip
mean/min/max, jitter RMS, peak deviation, detection counts, drift), and LATENCY BREAKDOWN. Then a
one-word verdict and a scrolling trace.

THE BREAKDOWN IS THE PART WORTH GETTING RIGHT, and the rule it follows is that a term is drawn only
if it is known:

  Schedule lead          exact, ours - MS_LOOKAHEAD_MS
  Audio input path       NOT knowable from inside a plug-in. The host owns the input device, and
                         VST3 tells a plug-in nothing about its latency. Drawn as "not measured"
                         with an empty bar until an audio loopback supplies it.
  Device MIDI to audio   what is left of the measured round trip once the above is taken out - and
                         while the input path is unknown it is labelled "Device + input path"
                         rather than "Device", because a figure called device latency that silently
                         contains an interface is exactly the overclaim this panel exists to avoid

Zero and "not measured" are different statements, and a panel that renders them the same way is
lying by omission. The bars make the dominant term obvious at a glance, which is the whole reason
for showing a breakdown rather than one number.

The trace is DEVIATION from the mean, not absolute latency: the absolute figure is a constant tens
of milliseconds tall and would flatten everything interesting into a straight line. One point per
detection rather than per block, since it is a picture of what the hardware did.

THE PROCESSOR-TO-EDITOR CHANNEL is GenBridge's status-slot mechanism, ported for the same reason it
exists there: with two instances loaded, a single global would have every editor reading whichever
processor wrote last. The processor claims a slot, sends the number over IConnectionPoint, and the
figures are then read straight out of the shared structure - a message per frame per instance would
be a great deal of allocation on a UI timer for numbers that are only ever advisory.

mstDriver now implements a minimal IHostApplication, because an IMessage can only be made by the
host: a driver passing nullptr as the initialise() context never exercises that path at all, and the
first place it would be tried would otherwise be Ableton. Verified: "controller connected", "editor
view created", "controller: status slot 0".

Building the panel needed freetype, which was not built in this project's own SynthLib checkout -
the nested ThirdParty submodules had never been initialised here. Recipe unchanged, in
G2-Edit/Docs/Third Party build notes.txt.

2026-09-02  THE PANEL'S CONTROLS, and why the environment variables had to go
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The plug-in loaded in Live, drew every figure correctly, and generated no clock at all. A HOST
LAUNCHED FROM THE DOCK INHERITS NO SHELL ENVIRONMENT, so every MST_* variable this was developed
against was empty inside it: MST_MIDI_DEST resolved to nothing, clock.destination stayed -1 and the
generator returned immediately. G2-Edit's plug-in notes record the identical trap with
$G2_VST3_PATCH, and this is the second time it has been paid for.

Three real VST3 parameters now: MIDI destination (a list), latency compensation (0-100 ms), and
which channel the detector analyses (left / right / left+right). The panel has a drop-down for the
two lists and a stepper for the compensation, drawn with SynthLib's contextMenu - which scrolls
itself once the list will not fit, so an unbounded port count is safe.

A LIST PARAMETER'S STEP COUNT IS FIXED AT REGISTRATION and cached by the host, so it cannot follow
how many ports the machine has. Slot count is MS_MIDI_MAX_DEST and only the number of slots pointing
at something real varies - GenBridge's lesson, taken rather than repeated.

THE PORT IS SAVED BY NAME, NOT BY INDEX, and that was not a precaution: it was measured. The Tempest
was destination index 6 with a Kronos and a Hydrasynth powered up and index 4 with them off. A
project storing the index would open a different instrument on a different day, silently. When the
saved name is absent the panel says "waiting for <name>" rather than falling back to nothing chosen -
the two are different states and rendering them alike is how a saved setup starts driving the wrong
port.

BUFFER SIZE IS REPORTED, NOT SETTABLE. A hosted plug-in does not own it. It is shown anyway because
it is the largest exactly-knowable part of the input path, and the breakdown now subtracts it before
naming the remainder.

THAT TERM WAS THEN CONFIRMED BY ACCIDENT. Two runs of the same rig differing only in the QU-24's
buffer:

  512 frames    round trip 21.90 ms
  256 frames    round trip 16.55 ms
  difference     5.35 ms, against a predicted 5.33

So the audio in a block really is captured one full buffer before the block arrives, and the "Host
buffer" row in the breakdown is exact rather than an estimate. What remains unseparated is the
interface's A/D and the device itself, which is why that row is labelled "Device + interface" until
a loopback splits them.

mstDriver gained a minimal IParameterChanges and --param ID VALUE, so the route that replaced the
environment variables is exercised offline rather than first tried in Live.

2026-09-02  DRIFT, MADE READABLE - and what it now actually measures
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The figure jumped about too much to read, and the cause was quantisation rather than noise. It was
computed from the TICK COUNT, and ticks are integers about 19 ms apart: one tick either way across a
five-second window is some 3800 ppm.

Counted in FRAMES instead, which is the audio clock itself and has no such step. That also changes
what the number means, for the better and precisely: it is now the host's AUDIO CLOCK against the
SYSTEM CLOCK. Blocks arrive at the interface's sample rate while the timestamps this plug-in hands
CoreMIDI are mach time, so this is exactly the quantity that decides whether a generated clock stays
with the music over a long session. GenBridge measures the same quantity between two devices and
steers a ring on it.

Measured on the QU-24, stable to about 1 ppm across a 46-second run:

  -19.0, -18.9, -19.1, -20.0, -19.0, -19.8 ppm

So its sample clock runs about 19 ppm slow against the Mac's - an ordinary crystal offset, and the
first genuinely physical number this tool has produced about the interface rather than about itself.

TWO THINGS HAD TO BE FIXED BEFORE IT SETTLED.

A LONG WINDOW OR NOTHING. Below about 30 seconds the reading is dominated by whatever the scheduler
last did rather than by any drift, so the panel now shows the window filling - "measuring - 12 of
30 s" - instead of a number that swings. Showing a figure and hoping the user discounts it is not a
design.

AND THE ANCHOR BLOCK'S FRAMES MUST NOT BE COUNTED. The anchor's wall time is taken at the START of a
block, so counting the frames that block goes on to deliver puts one whole block of audio time
against no wall time at all. It showed as a drift decaying like 1/t - 158 ppm at 30 seconds, 138 at
34 - which is the signature of a fixed offset being amortised rather than of any real drift. One
256-frame block is 5.33 ms, and 5.33 ms over 30 s is 178 ppm, which is the size of what was seen.

THE PANEL WAS ALSO TOO SHORT FOR ITS OWN CONTENT: about 740 units of rows in a 600-unit canvas, so
the graph fell off the bottom of the window. The canvas is now 740 AND the graph is anchored to the
canvas bottom rather than to the running y - so a row added above it can crowd the layout but can
never push it off again. A collision is visible; a control drawn past the edge is not.

The telemetry log line was gated on ticks having been sent, which meant a run with no destination
chosen produced no telemetry at all - exactly the run someone diagnosing a silent plug-in would be
looking at. Gated on the window instead.
